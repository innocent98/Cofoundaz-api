A launch week is a short sprint with a number to hit. It gives you energy and data.

## Set the target

Choose one measurable goal, such as **10 paying customers**, **50 signups** or **15 booked demos**. Work backward. If one in five conversations turns into a customer, you need about 50 conversations.

## Plan each day

A simple pattern:

- **Day 1:** tell your closest network. Send 20 personal messages.
- **Day 2:** post in two relevant communities with a useful, not salesy, message.
- **Day 3:** follow up with everyone who showed interest. Most sales happen on the follow up.
- **Day 4:** ask your first users for a quote or a short testimonial and an introduction.
- **Day 5:** try a second channel or a partner.
- **Day 6:** answer every question publicly and write down the objections.
- **Day 7:** review the numbers and decide what to repeat.

## Track simply

Keep a small table with the date, channel, how many people were contacted, how many replied and how many signed up or paid. Fancy tools are not needed.

## Do the review

At the end, ask three questions: What worked? What surprised us? What do we do next week? The answers form the plan for week two.

Launch is not a one time event. The founders who succeed treat the first week as the start of a rhythm of outreach, follow up and learning.
