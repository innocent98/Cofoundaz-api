A channel is a place where your customers already pay attention. You do not need ten. Pick two.

## Start with where your people are

Look back at your interviews. Where do these customers look for solutions? Common places for early products in Nigeria and across Africa include:

- **WhatsApp groups and status**, which are often the main business channel for small traders and service providers
- **Instagram and TikTok** for consumer and creative businesses
- **LinkedIn and email** for B2B and professional services
- **Communities and trade associations**, for example market unions, alumni networks and professional bodies
- **Partnerships** with a business that already serves your customers
- **Direct visits**, which still work well for local services

## Score each channel

For every channel you consider, give a score from 1 to 5 on three questions:

1. Are my customers actually there?
2. Can I reach 100 of them in a week without paying for ads?
3. Do I already know how to use this channel?

Add the scores. Take the top two.

## Why two and not five

A small team that spreads across five channels does each one badly and learns nothing. Two channels give you comparison: you can see which one brings people who stay.

## Write it down

Record each channel with a weekly target, for example *"20 personal messages and 3 posts in three WhatsApp groups per week."* Targets turn a channel into a habit you can measure.
