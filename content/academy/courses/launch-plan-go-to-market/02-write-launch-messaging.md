People decide in seconds whether something is for them. Your message must work in that time.

## A simple formula

**For [who], who [problem], [product] is a [category] that [main benefit]. Unlike [alternative], it [difference].**

Example: *For salon owners who lose bookings in crowded WhatsApp chats, Zuri is a booking assistant that confirms appointments in two taps. Unlike a notebook, it never forgets a client.*

Then shorten it to one line for posts and introductions: *"Never lose a salon booking in your WhatsApp chats again."*

## Use your customers' words

Open your interview notes and find the exact phrases people used about their problem. Your best headline is often a sentence someone already said.

## Pass the five second test

Show your one line to a stranger for five seconds, then hide it and ask: "What does this do, and who is it for?" If they cannot say, simplify.

## Match the message to the channel

A WhatsApp message should be short, personal and end with one question or link. A LinkedIn post can tell a short story. An email needs a clear subject line. Keep the same promise and change the form.

## One call to action

Every message should ask for one thing: reply, book a call, try the link. Messages with three asks get zero responses.

Write your formula, your one line and your single call to action on one page. You will use it all through launch week.
