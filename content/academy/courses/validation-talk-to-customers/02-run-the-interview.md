The single rule of a customer interview: **ask about the past, not the future.** People are poor at predicting what they will do and generous with compliments. They are far better at describing what they already did.

## A simple structure for 20 minutes

1. **Warm up (2 min).** Thank them, explain you are learning, and say there are no wrong answers.
2. **Their world (5 min).** "Tell me about your work. What does a normal week look like?"
3. **The last time (8 min).** "Tell me about the last time you ran into [the problem]. What happened? What did you do next? What did that cost you?"
4. **Current solutions (3 min).** "What do you use today? What do you like or hate about it? Have you ever paid for something to help?"
5. **Close (2 min).** "Who else should I talk to?"

## Questions to avoid

- "Would you use an app that does X?" People say yes to be polite.
- "How much would you pay?" It produces made-up numbers.
- Any question that describes your idea.

## Habits that help

- **Stay quiet after they answer.** Count to three. People often add the real answer in the pause.
- **Follow the energy.** If their voice changes when they mention something, ask "tell me more about that".
- **Write down exact words.** Their phrases become your marketing copy later.

Take notes right after each call while it is fresh. Keep the rule: no pitch until the call ends, and even then only if they ask.
