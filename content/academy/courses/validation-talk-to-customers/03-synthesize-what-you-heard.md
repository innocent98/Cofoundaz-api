Five interviews produce a pile of notes. Synthesis turns the pile into decisions.

## Step 1: pull out facts, not opinions

Go through each set of notes and mark only what the person did, spent, or said happened. "I would love that" is an opinion. "I spent three Saturdays reconciling this by hand last month" is a fact.

## Step 2: look for patterns across people

Make a simple table with one row per person and columns for the problem they described, what they do today, what it costs, and any strong phrase. Then look down each column:

- A pattern seen in **three or more** of five people is real enough to act on.
- A one-off story is interesting but not yet a pattern.

## Step 3: decide

Write three short statements:

1. **What we learned is true** (backed by at least three people).
2. **What we thought but did not see** (assumptions that no one confirmed).
3. **What we do next** (another round of interviews, a change to the problem statement, or a small test).

## Be ready to be wrong

Sometimes the honest finding is that people shrug at the problem. That is a good outcome, because it costs you a week and not a year. Change your problem statement, or choose a different group, and run the cycle again.

Log the interviews and the patterns in the Validation Hub so the whole team can see what the evidence says.
