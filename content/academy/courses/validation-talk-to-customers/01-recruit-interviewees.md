The quality of your interviews depends on who you talk to. Five people who truly have the problem teach you more than twenty who are merely polite.

## Who qualifies

Use your problem statement as a filter. If you wrote about salon owners who take bookings on WhatsApp, then a salon owner who uses a booking app already is a different segment. Decide on purpose.

## How to ask

Keep the message short, specific and free of selling:

*"Hi Tola, I am researching how salon owners manage bookings. I am not selling anything. Could I ask you 4 or 5 questions for 20 minutes this week? I can call at a time that suits you."*

Why this works: it names the topic, promises no pitch, states the time cost and gives the other person control.

## Where to find them

Ask each interviewee at the end: "Who else deals with this that I should talk to?" Introductions from a peer get far more replies than cold messages. Trade groups and WhatsApp communities work too, if you contribute before you ask.

## Small thank-yous

For a 20 minute call, a small airtime or data top-up, or lunch if you meet in person, is usually enough. Do not offer your product free as payment. That biases the conversation.

## Target

Book five conversations in your first week. Expect that about half of the people you message will answer. Message ten.
