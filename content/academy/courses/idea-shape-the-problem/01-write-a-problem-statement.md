A problem statement names a person, a situation and a cost. Use this shape:

**[Who] struggles to [do something] because [obstacle], which costs them [time, money or stress].**

Here is a real-feeling example: *Small pharmacy owners in Lagos struggle to track which drugs are about to expire because they use paper registers, so they write off stock every month.*

Notice what is missing. The word "app" never appears. A problem statement never mentions your solution, because the moment it does you stop looking at the problem.

## Three tests

- **Specific:** can you picture one actual person who fits? If you cannot, the "who" is too broad. "Small businesses" fails. "Salon owners who take bookings on WhatsApp" passes.
- **Observable:** could you watch it happen? You should be able to stand in a shop or sit in an office and see the struggle.
- **Costly:** does it cost something you can name, in naira, hours or stress?

## Exercise

Write five versions of your statement, each for a different type of person. Pick the version where you can already name three people who fit. If none of the five passes, that is useful news. It means the idea needs more looking before any building.

Keep the winning sentence. You will use it in every lesson that follows.
