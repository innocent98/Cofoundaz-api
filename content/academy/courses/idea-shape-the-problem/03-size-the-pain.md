Not every real problem is worth a business. This lesson helps you judge how much the problem hurts, and whether people already pay to fix it.

## Three signals of real pain

- **Frequency:** how often does it happen? A problem that appears daily is worth more than one that appears yearly.
- **Cost:** what does one occurrence cost? Put a number on it, even a rough one. Two hours lost, a ₦15,000 delivery that fails, a customer who walks away.
- **Existing effort:** what are people doing about it today? Workarounds are the strongest signal. If someone pays a freelancer, keeps a messy spreadsheet or hires a runner to cope, the problem is already a budget line.

## Do the arithmetic

Take your best guess for one person, then scale it modestly:

*A pharmacy writes off about ₦40,000 of expired stock per month. Across 12 months that is ₦480,000 per pharmacy. If a tool could halve that loss, the value is about ₦240,000 a year.*

If the yearly value to the customer is small, they will not pay much. If it is large and frequent, you have something worth validating properly.

## What to do next

Keep your numbers in a one page note with three lines: frequency, cost per occurrence, and what people do today. After your first conversations, replace each guess with something a real person told you. The next course shows you how to run those conversations.
