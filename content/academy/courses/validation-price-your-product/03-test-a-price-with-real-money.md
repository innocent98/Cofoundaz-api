Asking people "would you pay?" does not tell you much. Asking them to actually pay does.

## The smallest real test

Pick one of these:

- **Pre-sale.** Offer the product before it exists at a founding customer price, with a clear delivery date. Collect payment through a Paystack or Flutterwave payment link, or a bank transfer.
- **Paid pilot.** Offer a four week trial for a small fee. Deliver by hand if you must.
- **Invoice test.** For B2B, send a short proposal with a real price and see who replies.

## What counts as success

Decide the bar before you start. For example: *"If 3 of 10 qualified prospects pay, we keep the price. If 1 of 10 pays, we revisit it. If 0 pay, we change the offer, not just the price."*

## Read the objections

Write down the reasons people give. Different reasons point to different problems:

- "Too expensive" may mean the value was not clear, not that the number is wrong.
- "Let me think about it" often means they did not feel the pain.
- "Can I get it for less?" is a signal that the price is workable. Be ready with an answer that protects your price, such as a smaller scope at a smaller fee.

## Keep a pricing log

Record each offer, the price, the response and the reason. After ten conversations you will know more than any pricing article can tell you. Return to this log whenever you consider changing your price.
