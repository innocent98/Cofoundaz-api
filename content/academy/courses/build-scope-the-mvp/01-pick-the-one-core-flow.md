Your product promises one outcome. The core flow is the shortest path a customer takes to reach it.

## Write the promise

Complete this sentence: **"A customer can ___ in under ___ minutes."** For example: *"A salon owner can accept and confirm a booking in under two minutes."*

## Map the path

List every step from the customer's first contact to the promised result. Be literal. For the booking example:

1. Customer sends a message
2. Owner sees available slots
3. Owner confirms a slot
4. Customer gets a confirmation

That four step path is your core flow. Everything else, such as reports, loyalty points and staff scheduling, is not part of it.

## Ask three questions about each step

- Does the promise break without it?
- Can it be done by hand for now?
- Can a tool you already have do it, such as WhatsApp, a form or a spreadsheet?

## Build only what must be software

Many early founders find that only one or two steps need real code. The rest can be manual at the start. This is not cheating. It is called a concierge approach, and it teaches you what the software should do before you write it.

## Output of this lesson

A single page with the promise, the steps, and a mark beside each step: **build**, **manual** or **use existing tool**. Share it with one customer and ask: "If this worked, would it solve your problem?" Adjust until the answer is a clear yes.
