Founders rarely run out of ideas. They run out of time and money. Cutting is the main skill of scoping.

## Use a clear rule

For each feature on your wish list, ask: **"Can a customer get the promised result without it?"** If yes, it is out of version one. Do not debate. Move it to a "later" list.

## Sort what remains

Mark every remaining item with one of three tags:

- **Must:** the core flow breaks without it.
- **Should:** it makes the flow smoother, but customers can live without it.
- **Could:** nice to have.

Version one contains only the **musts**. Take one honest look at whether a **should** is actually a must in disguise, and then stop.

## Common things to cut

- Custom user roles and permissions
- Advanced dashboards and reports
- Settings pages for every option
- Multiple languages, unless your first customers require it
- Native apps when a mobile friendly web page works

## Protect against scope creep

Write the list of cut items down and keep it visible. When someone asks "can we just add...", point to the list and ask which must item they want to replace. A feature added without something removed is a delay in disguise.

## A useful test

If you feel slightly embarrassed by how small version one is, you are probably close. Customers forgive missing features far more easily than a product that arrives six months late and solves the wrong problem.
