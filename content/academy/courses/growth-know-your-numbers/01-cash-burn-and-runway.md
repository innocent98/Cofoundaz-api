Three numbers tell you how long your business can survive.

## Cash

Cash is the money you can spend today: bank balances plus mobile money and cash on hand. It is not revenue you have invoiced but not collected, and it is not profit.

## Burn

Burn is how much cash you lose each month.

- **Gross burn:** everything you spend per month, including salaries, tools, rent, data and transport.
- **Net burn:** gross burn minus the cash you receive from customers.

Example: you spend ₦1,200,000 a month and collect ₦400,000. Net burn is **₦800,000** a month.

## Runway

**Runway = cash ÷ net burn.**

If you have ₦4,000,000 in the bank, runway is 4,000,000 ÷ 800,000 = **5 months**.

## What to do with it

- Know your runway at all times. Check it every week.
- Fundraising and sales take longer than you expect. Start raising or fixing revenue when you have **at least six months** of runway, not two.
- If runway is short, you have three levers: cut spending, raise more revenue or raise money. Pick one on purpose.

## Keep it honest

Include upcoming costs that are not monthly, such as annual software, taxes and equipment. Remove customer payments you are not sure will arrive. A cautious runway is a more useful runway.

Update the figure every month and keep it where the whole team can see it.
