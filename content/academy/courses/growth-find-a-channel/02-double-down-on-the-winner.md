Once one channel clearly beats the others, the job changes from testing to scaling it carefully.

## Check that it is really working

Before you invest more, confirm three things:

1. The customers it brought **stay**. Look at who is still active after a month.
2. The cost per customer is **well below** what a customer is worth to you.
3. You can **explain why** it works. If you cannot, you cannot repeat it.

## Increase in steps

Raise effort by about 30 to 50 percent and watch the result for a full cycle. If the cost per customer holds steady, raise it again. If it rises sharply, you have found the channel's limit for now, and it is time to test a new one beside it.

## Systemise what works

Write down the steps, templates and timing that produce results so that someone else can run them. A good playbook is a one page document with the messages, the follow up schedule and the numbers to watch.

## Protect the product

Fast growth stresses support, delivery and quality. Set a minimum standard, such as replying to every new customer within a day, and slow the channel down if you break it. A churned customer costs more than a delayed one.

## Keep one test running

Even while you scale the winner, run a small test of a new channel each month. Channels tire over time, and you want the next one ready before you need it.
