If you do not know what a customer costs you to win, you cannot tell whether growth is healthy.

## Customer acquisition cost

**CAC = total spent on acquiring customers ÷ number of new customers in the same period.**

Include everything: ads, tools, discounts, commissions, and the time of people who sell, valued at a fair rate. Founders often forget their own time. A channel that looks free but takes twenty hours a week is not free.

Example: ₦150,000 on ads and referral rewards plus ₦100,000 of sales time, 25 new customers. CAC is ₦250,000 ÷ 25 = **₦10,000**.

## Compare with what a customer is worth

If a customer pays ₦8,000 a month and stays 10 months on average, the revenue is ₦80,000. Subtract the cost of serving them and you get the margin you actually keep. CAC should be a small fraction of that margin. A common target is for lifetime margin to be at least three times CAC, but use your own market's reality.

## Watch the payback time

How many months of a customer's payments does it take to recover CAC? In the example, ₦10,000 ÷ ₦8,000 is about 1.3 months of revenue. Shorter payback means you can reinvest faster.

## Track it by channel

Calculate CAC for each channel separately. The average hides the truth: one channel may be excellent and another quietly burning cash.
