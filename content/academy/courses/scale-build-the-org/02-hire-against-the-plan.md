Hiring is the biggest spending decision most startups make. It should follow the work, not the mood of the month.

## Start from the work

List the tasks that are not getting done or that take too much of your own time. Group them into roles. Ask for each role:

- What outcome should this person deliver in 90 days?
- What would we stop doing if we hired them?
- Can a contractor, a part time helper or a better tool do this for now?

## Check the money

Calculate the full monthly cost: salary, pension and tax contributions, equipment, data, training and management time. Compare it with your runway from the numbers course. Never hire so that runway falls under six months unless the role directly brings in revenue.

## Write the role clearly

A good brief has the outcomes, the first 90 days, the skills that truly matter and the working hours. Avoid long lists of "nice to haves". Describe the problems the person will solve.

## Run a fair, simple process

1. A short call to check basic fit
2. A small paid task that mirrors the real work
3. A conversation with the team
4. Reference checks

Use the same questions for every candidate, and write notes immediately after each conversation.

## Start with a trial and clear expectations

Agree a probation period and goals in writing. Meet weekly for the first month. Fast, kind feedback early is the best way to help a new hire succeed, or to part ways before it becomes costly.
