Unclear promises about ownership cause more broken teams than almost any other problem. Settle them early and put them in writing.

*This lesson is general guidance, not legal advice. Use a qualified lawyer for the documents.*

## Roles first

Write down who is responsible for what. Name a final decision maker for each area, such as product, sales, finance and operations. Clear ownership prevents two people from quietly waiting for each other.

## Equity in plain terms

Equity is a share of ownership. When you offer it, decide:

- **How much** for each person, based on their role, risk and contribution
- **Vesting:** equity earned over time, commonly across four years, so that someone who leaves early does not walk away with a large stake
- **A cliff:** a first period, often one year, before any equity is earned
- **What happens when someone leaves,** including whether the company can buy back unvested shares

## Put it in writing

Handshake deals fade. Agree on a written founders' agreement and employee or advisor agreements that cover equity, roles, intellectual property and confidentiality. Make sure what each person builds belongs to the company.

## Keep records tidy

Maintain a simple record of who owns what, when it vests and which documents were signed. Investors will ask for it, and it also protects the team.

## Revisit as you grow

As roles change, review the agreement. If a founder's role shrinks or grows, discuss it openly before it becomes resentment. A short, honest conversation now costs far less than a dispute later.

Before signing, speak to a lawyer who understands company law in your country.
