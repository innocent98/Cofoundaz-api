If the business only works when you are in the room, it cannot grow past you. Documenting processes is how you move knowledge out of your head.

## Pick the processes that matter

List the five things that must happen reliably for customers to get value and for you to get paid. Typical examples: onboarding a new customer, delivering the product or service, handling a complaint, collecting payment and paying suppliers or staff.

## Write them as short checklists

For each process, write:

- **The trigger:** what starts it
- **The steps:** numbered, in order, each with an owner
- **The output:** what "done" looks like
- **The tools and files** needed
- **Common mistakes** and how to fix them

Use plain words and a page or less. A process nobody reads is worse than none, so keep it short.

## Write them with the people who do the work

The person doing the task knows the real steps. Sit with them, watch the work and write what actually happens, then improve it together.

## Make them easy to find

Store processes in one shared place, such as a shared drive or the Documents area of Cofoundaz. Name each file clearly and add the date of the last update.

## Review every quarter

Processes decay as the business changes. Put a quarterly reminder to review and update them, and ask each new hire to improve one process in their first month. That is how documents stay alive.
