A first price is a hypothesis, not a promise. You will change it. The goal is to start close enough to learn quickly.

## Three inputs

1. **The alternative.** What do customers pay today, in money and in time, to solve the problem? Compare yourself to that, not to a competitor's website alone.
2. **The value.** How much money or time do you save or earn the customer? Estimate it in naira.
3. **Your costs.** The price must at least cover the cost of serving a customer, with a margin. If it cannot, the model needs work.

## A starting range

Many founders begin with a price at roughly 10 to 30 percent of the value they create, and near or under the alternative. If a tool saves a business ₦80,000 a month, a price between ₦8,000 and ₦24,000 is a reasonable test range.

## Make paying easy

Customers in your market may prefer bank transfer, card or mobile payment. Offer at least two ways. A monthly plan with a small discount for a yearly payment suits both cautious and committed buyers.

## Test with real offers

Ask ten qualified people to pay. Record the price, the answer and the reason. If nearly everyone accepts at once, the price is probably too low. If almost nobody accepts and the reasons mention value, work on the offer before cutting the price.

## Changing a price

Raise prices for new customers first. Tell existing customers early, explain the reason and thank them for being first. Avoid frequent changes in the same quarter.
