A scope document does not need to be long. One page is enough, and it keeps everyone honest.

## What goes on the page

1. **The promise.** One sentence: "A customer can ___ in under ___ minutes."
2. **The customer.** Who exactly is version one for?
3. **The core flow.** The numbered steps from first contact to result.
4. **Must have list.** Only what the core flow cannot work without.
5. **Not now list.** Everything you decided to leave out, so no one asks again.
6. **Manual steps.** The parts you will do by hand at first.
7. **Done means.** What the customer can do and what you can measure.
8. **Date.** When you ship it to the first users.

## How to use it

- Share the page with two or three target customers and ask: "If this worked, would it solve your problem?"
- Review it weekly. Cross off finished items. Anything new must replace something on the must list.
- Keep the Not now list. It often becomes the roadmap for version two.

## Sizing the work

Break each must item into tasks that take at most two days. If something takes longer, it is probably two things. Count the tasks, estimate honestly and add a buffer of about 30 percent for surprises.

## A realistic goal

Aim to put the first version in front of real customers within four to eight weeks. If your plan takes longer, cut again. Learning from real use beats polishing an unused product.
