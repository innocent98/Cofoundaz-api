Your first hire changes the company. A little writing before you start prevents expensive mistakes.

## 1. The job outcomes

List three results this person should deliver in 90 days. Outcomes beat tasks. "Handle all customer replies within 24 hours" is clearer than "do customer support".

## 2. The real cost

Add salary, taxes and pension contributions, equipment, data and training. Compare it with your runway. A hire that brings your runway under six months needs a strong reason, such as bringing in revenue.

## 3. The alternatives

Could a freelancer, an intern, a part time helper or a better tool do the job for now? A short trial with a contractor is a cheap way to learn whether the role is needed.

## 4. A first month plan

Write what the person will learn, who they will meet and what they will deliver by the end of week one, week two and week four. New hires do better with a clear start.

## 5. The way you give feedback

Decide how you will check progress: a weekly one to one, a short written update, or both. Plan to give feedback early and kindly.

## Hire for the work you have

Describe the actual problems, not a list of wishes. Test candidates with a small, paid task that looks like the work. It tells you more than a long interview.

## After you hire

Document your core processes so that the person can learn without you repeating yourself. Every hour spent on this is returned many times.
