Conversations about ownership are easiest at the start, when no one has much to lose. They get harder with every month of progress.

*This is general guidance, not legal advice. Use a lawyer for the written agreement.*

## Topics to cover

1. **Roles.** Who leads product, sales, money and operations? Who has the final say in each?
2. **Time and money.** Is everyone full time? Does anyone put in cash? How is it treated?
3. **Ownership split.** Based on role, risk, experience and contribution, not on who had the idea alone.
4. **Vesting.** Equity earned over time, often over four years, with a first period before anything vests. It protects the team if someone leaves early.
5. **Leaving.** What happens to unvested shares? Can the company buy back vested shares, and at what price?
6. **Decisions.** Which decisions need everyone to agree, such as raising money, selling the company or hiring a senior person?
7. **Intellectual property.** Everything built for the company belongs to the company.

## How to run the talk

Set a time with no distractions. Each person writes their expectations first, then you compare. Disagreements are normal. The point is to find them early.

## Put it in writing

Capture the result in a founders' agreement drafted or reviewed by a lawyer who knows company law where you operate. A shared note is a good start, but it is not enough once there is real value.

## Revisit it

Review the agreement each year or when roles change. Honest check ins prevent quiet resentment.
