The best customer questions are about the past. People are poor at predicting their own behaviour and very good at describing what they already did.

## Questions worth asking

1. **"Tell me about the last time you [had this problem]."** Start here every time. The story reveals the steps, the people involved and the emotions.
2. **"What did you do next?"** This shows the real workaround, not the one people claim.
3. **"What did that cost you?"** Time, money or a lost customer. Let them name the number.
4. **"What have you tried to fix it?"** Past effort is the strongest evidence of real pain.
5. **"What did you like or hate about that?"** This shows what a good solution must do.
6. **"Why was that important to you?"** Ask it when an answer sounds like a slogan.
7. **"Who else should I talk to?"** It gives you introductions and shows who else cares.

## Questions to avoid

- **"Would you use this?"** People say yes to be kind.
- **"How much would you pay?"** The answer is a guess.
- **"Do you think this is a good idea?"** You will hear compliments, not facts.
- **Leading questions** such as "Don't you find it annoying when...?"

## Two habits that change the result

- **Be quiet after they answer.** The real answer often comes in the pause.
- **Write their exact words.** A phrase like "I just lose track" can become your headline.

## Quick check after a call

Did you hear a specific story? A number? A thing they paid for? If the call contained only opinions about the future, schedule a follow up and ask about the past.
