Customers buy painkillers on the same day. They think about vitamins for months and often never buy. Early founders need painkillers.

## Five questions to check your idea

1. **Is the problem urgent?** Does it get in the way of money, time or reputation this week?
2. **Is anyone already spending effort or money on it?** A workaround is a good sign.
3. **Can you name three people who would give you a call back today?** If not, the pain may be small.
4. **Does it cost something clear?** A number in naira or hours makes pain real.
5. **Would they be upset if your product disappeared after three months?** This is a strong test once you have users.

## Score yourself honestly

Give one point for each yes. Four or five points suggest a painkiller. Two or fewer suggest a vitamin. A vitamin can still become a business, but it will be harder to sell and slower to grow.

## What to do with a vitamin

- Narrow the audience until you find a group where the pain is sharp.
- Attach the idea to a moment of urgency, such as tax time, a busy season or a new regulation that affects your customers.
- Look for the painkiller hiding inside the idea. Sometimes one feature solves the urgent problem, and the rest can wait.

## A reminder

Excitement from friends does not measure pain. Look for behaviour: people who switch, pay, complain or ask when it will be ready.
